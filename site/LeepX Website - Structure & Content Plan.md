# LeepX Website — Structure & Content Plan

2026-09-18 · @Someone

## Fold 1 — Hero

- Intro video stays in the first fold
- Headline is changing away from a "getting stuck" framing toward an opportunity/FOMO metaphor — e.g. "why haven't you opened your doors yet — when will you walk in?"
- One word — "मिट्टी" (mitti/soil) — came up as a headline direction with no further explanation on the call. Needs confirming (see Open Items below).
- Supporting copy under the headline: 1–2 short phrases max, nothing longer

## Fold 2 — Trust / FOMO

- Decision: repurpose the existing large testimonial grid here rather than building a new "problems" section
- Framing: "others have already taken this step — when are you taking the lead?"
- Goal: by the end of fold 2, visitors should be convinced this is legitimate *and* feel FOMO that others are already in

## Fold 3 — Problem / Solution

- 4–5 problem statements: stuck in career, no international/dollar-client experience, tired of commuting, knows what to do but can't execute alone, needs a push to get started
- Paired with 4–5 tangible outcomes: clear roadmap, global-remote-ready CV, optimized LinkedIn, portfolio and projects, proof of work — framed as "you walk away with these regardless of which course you pick"
- Decided against a separate deep-dive case study section here — show it visually (problem/solution pairs), not as written copy
- One specific case study is being cut ("how we selected her" — wrong angle, doesn't show her landing a job); the other case studies stay

## "How It Works" / Journey Section

- Merge the two competing layout ideas (box roadmap vs. step-journey) into one section
- Flow: quick assessment → matched to a role/pathway → join the relevant cohort → start getting matched opportunities
- Add 2–3 in-cohort steps plus some "checkpoint" green-flag moments during the program
- The box-based roadmap layout was preferred over the more text-heavy alternative (compared against Ali Abdaal's site and rejected as too text-heavy for this audience)

## Programs & Cohort Visibility

- Keep specific programs/cohorts subtle on the homepage — footer links or a small icon-only "ongoing programs" section, not a prominent block
- Reasoning: don't dilute focus on the primary CTA (the assessment); people who need program details can find them via footer or a dropdown
- If a LinkedIn-focused sub-site launches later, it needs its own light mention here too, without pulling attention away from the assessment flow

## Copy & Tone Guidelines

- Avoid vague, Western-startup-style language (benchmark: Ali Abdaal's Lifestyle Business Academy site was judged as not landing with this audience)
- Use direct, familiar terms instead: career switch, global opportunities, CV, LinkedIn optimization, outreach strategy, portfolio, projects
- Rule of thumb: if the audience wouldn't recognize the word from everyday conversation, replace it

## Testimonials

- Don't clump testimonials in one block — sprinkle short, snapshot-style testimonials every couple of sections, including on the cover itself
- Long-form, text-heavy testimonial cards (Western style) were explicitly rejected — too much text for this audience
- Current snapshot-style layout was already well liked in review and just needs content swapped in

## Founders / About Section

- Minimal text, stat-box format rather than long bios
- Stat boxes: community size (500k+), mentee count (\~500–700+, to be confirmed with exact number), total value of packages/opportunities placed
- 2–3 credibility lines per founder — e.g. global freelance experience across Singapore, Europe, and US clients

## Hero Video Production

- Plan: roughly one minute of the founder speaking, intercut with brief clips of others, edited with music for a high-energy feel
- Reference point: check how Ali Abdaal and similar creators structure their own site intros before scripting
- Founder to script her own talking points after reviewing those references

## Explicitly Rejected

- A "free resources" section on the site — decision was that IG reels/free content should redirect *to* the site, not host resources that compete with it
- The "how we selected her" case study (wrong angle — see Fold 3)
- Long-form, text-heavy testimonial cards (see Testimonials)

## Free Community CTA

- Decision: yes, include a path to the free community (LeepX Academy), but keep it subtle rather than a primary CTA
- Reasoning: the primary CTA (the assessment) is meant to filter for market-qualified leads — a loud free-community link risks pulling attention from low-intent visitors with no paying capacity
- Keeping the door open for future low-ticket masterclasses aimed at that segment, so the CTA isn't removed entirely — just de-emphasized (people who look for it will find it)

## FAQ

- Confirmed keeper — the recurring questions people ask justify a dedicated FAQ section
